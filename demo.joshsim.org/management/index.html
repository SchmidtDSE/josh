From a forest to a managed landscape

In the intro demo we grew a forest of ForeverTrees from climate alone. Real landscapes also face competition, disturbance, and the management decisions people make in response. Here we start from that same forest and layer on three things: an invasive grass that competes with young trees, a fire that clears part of the map, and the management we can apply afterwards. As before, we build it up step by step and then run it — in your browser — so you can try the management choices yourself.


          

          

            

            

            

              
              
            

          

        

      


      

        

          

            
Try it yourself

            
Now that we have built up the simulation, give it a run. It executes on your computer
               in your browser. The interesting knobs are in the configuration below: how many trees
               to outplant after the fire, and whether to knock back the invasive grass. Change them
               and click Run to see how the burned, managed zone recovers.

            

            

              Configuration
              
              
Try outplantCount and removeInvasives:
                 planting adults and removing grass both help the fire scar revegetate.

            

            

              
              
              
              
            

          

          

            

            
Switch the variable above the map to follow the story: after
               the fire, burned trees spike and invasive cover
               climbs in the cleared cells; with outplanting and removal on, adult
               and live tree counts recover inside the managed zone while cover
               stays low.

            
Climate inputs driving this simulation

            

              
              
              
              
            

            
The same climate as the intro demo (≈3 km grid)
               drives growth on the 16 km model patches; fire and management then reshape where
               trees survive.
